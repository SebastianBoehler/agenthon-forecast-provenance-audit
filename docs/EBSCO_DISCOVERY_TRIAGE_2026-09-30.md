### Scope and contribution consequences

**RewardHackingAgents — established adjacent integrity benchmark.** It compares
agent-reported and protected reference metrics, logs patches/file access, and tests
evaluator locking plus training-time test-access denial. Its tasks include credit
risk classification, image classification and sentiment; the exposure is mutable
evaluation infrastructure. [Author full text, §§III–VII](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11337v1).

This threatens claims to introduce evaluation integrity as a benchmark outcome or
paired reported/reference scores. It supports preserving evaluator code and held-out
access boundaries, and separating unexplained metric drift from evidenced tampering.
Our relevant distinction is externally released supervision with financial quantity,
visibility and rounding defects in a fixed comparison rule. That distinction alone
does not earn method novelty or show learning harm. Their protected reference is a
trusted implementation for their threat model, not a financial-semantic oracle.

**Measurement Risk — direct financial measurement overlap.** Its 253-item Japanese
Financial Implicit-Commitment Recognition test crosses four models, five rubrics,
three temperatures and five metrics. It reports rubric-sensitive labels and ranking
conclusions, screens weak metrics, and pins an artifact while diagnosing dataset-card
row-count drift. Rubric changes confound semantics, examples and verbosity; it avoids
causal linguistic claims. [Preprint abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27374v2),
[full text, §§1.2, 3.2, 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.27374v2).

This threatens first financial measurement, ranking-sensitivity or provenance-audit
claims. Our evidence traces released numeric/preference targets that fail visible
financial quantity and rounding contracts. Compare any proposed audit discipline
against this framework. Neither its classification results nor our format-limited
scalar panel establishes general financial ability or downstream harm.

**Backtest — database-abstract-limited financial auditor benchmark.** The author
abstract in EBSCO record `edseee.11692419` describes 141 cases across identifier
validity, filing timestamps, accounting availability and universe construction.
It compares general/specialized LLM auditor prompts, multiple models and rules, and
reports tradeoffs between falsely accepting invalid workflows and falsely rejecting
valid ones. These are abstract-reported scope and findings, not independently
verified empirical results; IEEE full text remains unread.

This establishes a substantive collision with broad first-benchmark claims about
LLM financial auditing or point-in-time provenance checking. It supports evaluating
both false acceptance and false rejection rather than reporting accuracy alone.
The abstract does not establish overlap with our traced released numeric/preference
label defects, generator–verifier coupling, visible-input answer determinacy or
adaptation. Exact construction and method overlap still require the full paper.

**AQER — existing evaluation method without observed gold.** AQER forms a response
proxy through similarity-based voting and iteratively reweights workers; LLM responses
are scored against human-worker proxies. Evaluations use computer-science and factual
question-answer data. The method assumes a correct answer exists. Sections 7.2 and
7.4 discuss failure under correlated errors and the unvalidated extension to
LLM-only response pools. [Publisher full text, §§4, 6–7](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/full/10.1287/isre.2023.0426).

This threatens a first no-ground-truth free-text/LLM quality-control claim. It supports
our need for independent evidence: consensus or shared generator outputs cannot
certify financial correctness. It supplies neither a finance-specific release audit
nor proof that a question with missing inputs has one identifiable numerical answer.
