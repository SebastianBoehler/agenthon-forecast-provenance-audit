## Executive summary (read this first)

Use the five-word title **When Verified Financial Labels Fail**. Optimize the paper
for a reader to identify its task, evidence and limits quickly. Do not claim that
a short title causally improves workshop acceptance.

## Evidence about title length

Letchford, Moat and Preis (2015), *The advantage of short paper titles*, analyse
the 20,000 most cited articles in each year from 2007 through 2013. Shorter titles
correlate with more citations in that selected sample. After accounting for
publication journal, associations are smaller and absent in some years. This is
an observational study of citations, not a randomized title intervention or an
analysis of conference acceptance.

Primary full text: https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/73234/1/WRAP_150266.full.pdf
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.150266

The practical decision is to remove the long subtitle and state the finding
directly. Its conditional word ``When'' avoids implying that every verified
financial label is wrong. The title retains searchable domain and supervision
keywords. No measured attention or acceptance gain is claimed for our title.

## Applied editorial choices

- Open with the wrong financial quantity, its independently checked value, and
  the generator/verifier coupling that explains agreement.
- State the natural-language task: identify the requested quantity, use the
  visible numbers, follow rounding and units, then inspect the grading decision.
- Lead model evidence with the frozen strict endpoint. Separate missing-unit
  sensitivity and the later size extension with their actual timing.
- Compare unchanged answers under different graders. Avoid presenting a higher
  acceptance rate as a higher model reasoning accuracy.
- Include passing families, formula/rounding ablations and the cheap integer
  baseline. Present within-family size and cross-family comparisons with the
  runtime, training and specialization confounds named.
- Keep numerical validity separate from reasoning quality and future learning
  effects. Human expert review remains outstanding.

The research contribution is a reproducible external audit of released financial
supervision and demonstrated grading consequences. A novel verification method,
a representative prevalence estimate and a training improvement are not claimed.
