### Relevant contribution and limits

*Post-Audit Mirage* constructs safe/harmful lifecycle pairs with identical public audit evidence and opposite protected outcomes. It operationalizes an existing indistinguishability argument across authorization, scheduling and shared-capacity environments, explicitly claiming “not a new theoretical principle” (physical PDF p. 2). A monitor uses fresh group outcomes and can abstain. Its guarantee needs stable, independent outcomes, a fixed group roster and outcome-independent missingness. Results concern synthetic panels, not production prevalence or general system rankings. The paper correctly treats “Each semantic lifecycle is one statistical unit” (p. 4), rather than counting nested streams as independent trials. [Pinned manuscript](https://github.com/beingamanforever/Post-Audit-Mirage/blob/66b8da150cd513d4340d16620e651b294a3b528c/paper.pdf).

### Implication for our financial audit

This overlaps a broad claim that more calculation cannot recover information absent from an audit, and demonstrates an executable artifact with explicit scorer boundaries. It does not investigate our released debt-for-call labels, whole-unit instruction failures or fixed-answer financial grading. Our own rounded-input feasibility cases should retain uncertainty; a hidden variable does not automatically imply a defective label. Our definite quantity/rounding results need their independent financial identities and requested output contracts.

The useful design lesson is to encode which evidence distinguishes candidate interpretations, report the true experimental unit, and retain undecided cases. A same-field future benchmark could contrast identifiable financial quantity errors with ambiguous visible-input conventions. It would need explicit contracts and held-out mechanisms; a generic matched-world theorem would add little novelty.
