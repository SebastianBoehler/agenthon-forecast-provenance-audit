\begin{abstract}
Released financial supervision can be marked verified while computing a different quantity from the one requested. We audit 12,655 rows from selected families of two public synthetic training releases. In Cosimo, 975 of 1,000 binomial-call labels disagree with independent valuation, while all 1,000 match financing debt and pass tested call-price bounds. Separately pinned public code computes debt and verifies by regenerating its template; the exact original dataset-generation revision is unproven. Another 946 of 1,000 constructed Gordon-growth labels violate requested integer rounding. Seven comparison families pass, while hidden-precision discrepancies remain feasible under rounded-input semantics.
% BEGIN GENERATED ABSTRACT_MODELS
Grading 800 unchanged answers from four models, reconstructed original-label cent-level scoring rejects 55 of 136 DeepSeek answers satisfying the strict final-line and numerical contract.
% END GENERATED ABSTRACT_MODELS
An additional comparator audit uses 368 scalar-rational MATH500 references and 96 local-model requests to separate normalization, extraction and financial target selection. Authored controls expose unequal-value credit and equivalent-answer rejection; the small natural mathematical panel shows no unequal-value credit. Released-label scoring denies 16 of 28 contract-valid local financial responses. A 300-instance independently authored FinChain-code comparison passes under source rounding, retaining 18 convention-dependent call-price differences rather than declaring new quantity errors. Document follow-ups retain a null quantity-reminder result and AI-only reference limitations. Our source-traced evidence package supplies pinned inputs, passing controls, independent arithmetic checks, numerical patches and an exploration history retaining failed branches. We claim neither a new verification principle, expert-certified reference set nor a measured effect on model training.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Consider a released, code-verified financial question: a one-period binomial call with spot price 58, up/down factors 1.15/0.85, strike 48, and risk-free rate 6\%. Independent portfolio replication and
risk-neutral valuation give 12.72. The released target is 45.28. This is the financing debt in the replicating portfolio, not the call price. The inspected verification code regenerates the same template:
agreement can preserve the generator's computational mistake. In the audited family, all 1,000 released labels match financing debt and pass the tested call-price bounds, although 975 fail call valuation. This numerical pattern is consistent with the separately pinned code; the exact original generation revision is unproven. The code diagnosis and independent valuation are documented separately~\cite{cosimosource,crr}.

These examples matter for synthetic financial training data because correctness metadata and preferred completions can become supervision. Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) uses
machine-checkable correctness signals; preference optimization uses chosen versus rejected responses. A target can be internally consistent with generator code while failing the visible question.

Each model reads a financial question and writes a calculation and final answer. We keep that response fixed and ask how different scoring rules grade it. This isolates grading decisions on the observed
answers; it does not measure whether training on the released data caused a model to learn an error.

Our research question is empirical: \emph{which released numeric and preference targets disagree with their visible financial questions, and how does using those targets change numerical grading?} We contribute
a pinned census of selected supervision families with passing comparisons, diagnosis in separately pinned public code, and paired reconstructed grading of unchanged model answers. The census comprises 12,655 rows and 10,476 distinct visible questions; repeated templates do not provide independent replications of the failure mechanisms. Precision validation, answer determinacy and fixed-answer grading sensitivity are established. Our empirical contribution is the release-specific quantity and output-instruction failures, their preference-label implications, and the resulting grading disagreements. A bounded cross-domain comparator audit further separates value equivalence from answer extraction and financial target selection; it is not a new verification algorithm.

\section{What the visible question specifies}
A question asking for a call price differs semantically from one asking for the debt used to replicate that call. Likewise, ``round to the nearest whole unit'' changes the requested answer even if the
underlying formula is correct. We call the numerical inputs, financial quantity, units and output instruction the visible-question contract. From a natural-language processing perspective, label correctness
depends on interpreting these words as well as performing arithmetic. Agreement with generator code tests a different property.

We distinguish three mechanisms. Rendering can hide input precision; computation can implement the wrong financial quantity; and answer serialization can omit a requested rounding step. If a prompt leaves the
input convention implicit, we report exact-input and conventional rounded-input interpretations separately. We do not silently assign uncertainty to every printed number.

For terminal value $F/(r-g)$, a cash flow of 740 and displayed rate spread of 6.5 percentage points determine 11,384.62 when the rates are exact. If each rate is rounded to 0.1 percentage point, values from
11,212.12 to 11,562.50 are feasible. A hidden target inside that range is possible but not uniquely determined by the displayed numbers. For whole-unit Gordon growth, we accept equivalent numeric
representations such as 31 and 31.0. At exact half ties, either adjacent integer is accepted because the original question specifies no tie convention.

\section{Data and experimental protocol}
\paragraph{Pinned sources.} Financial RLVR 10k Enterprise~\cite{rlvr} is pinned at revision
\texttt{6cfa9a71e777}. We include all 2,655 ordinary DCF rows, excluding the
release's explicitly marked edge cases. Cosimo CFA/FRM 71k~\cite{cosimo} is pinned at
\texttt{42244d29c6b9}; we audit ten named families in its CFA Level I split,
with 1,000 rows each. Both dataset cards declare MIT licenses. All selected rows carry verification metadata. Input SHA256 hashes are checked before loading, and parser failures stop the experiment. Full
revision hashes are in the artifact manifest.
Cosimo's public generator/verifier code is inspected at the separate revision
\texttt{20668622104b}; this code pin is not established as the original dataset-generation revision.

\paragraph{Selection and discovery status.} The DCF and constructed-response Gordon pilots and the first binomial example were known before the expanded
protocol was frozen. Family selection reflects discovered mechanisms and independently implementable formulas, rather than random sampling. CAPM's discrepancy was found during the expanded census. The study is
exploratory, with recorded protocol amendments; the freeze is not retrospective preregistration. There are 12,655 rows and 10,476 distinct visible questions when deduplicated within family. We report finite
counts without independent-row confidence intervals.

\paragraph{Independent numerical evaluation.} The primary implementation uses 50-digit Decimal arithmetic from visible operands. For cent-serialized answers
without an explicit precision instruction, the numerical allowance is 0.005 in the answer's units. Four-decimal serialization uses 0.00005. An additional $10^{-20}$ guard avoids numerical boundary artifacts.
Explicit integer instructions use the integer contract. Binomial calls are checked by both replication and risk-neutral expectation~\cite{crr}, using the declared one-period gross rate $1+r$; annuities and
present values also use independent cash-flow identities. Released code is read as source or abstract syntax trees, never executed.

AI agents assisted source inspection, implementation and manuscript preparation. A separately implemented rational-arithmetic review checks 5,935 rows: all DCF, CAPM, binomial and constructed-response Gordon cases, plus 40 deterministically selected cases from each of the remaining seven
families. This is AI-assisted technical validation, not human financial-expert annotation. Source inspection and numerical identities provide checks beyond rerunning the same implementation. The reviewer read
the primary code before implementing the rational calculations; the check is therefore technical validation, not a blinded conceptual replication. The actual exported numerical patches also agree with these
5,935 rational values.

\paragraph{Grading and repair conditions.} We reconstruct original-label equality; absolute tolerances 0.0001, 0.005, 0.5 and 1; and relative tolerances 0.1\%,
1\% and 5\%. These are explicit sensitivity experiments, not observed upstream rewards. The RLVR 0.0001 condition analytically follows its card's correctness specification; executable deployed reward code was
not reproduced. We test formula-faithful answers, targeted wrong-formula candidates, and the releases' actual rejected answers. Error candidates that coincide with a valid final value are reported separately,
rather than counted as false acceptances.

Corrections independently recompute visible inputs and apply requested rounding. The formula-only ablation retains cent-level numeric acceptance for the Gordon case. For hidden-precision families, numerical
patches explicitly add an exact-input convention; this changes the task specification rather than recovering unknowable hidden values. Patches contain repaired numerical labels and source IDs, not certified
reasoning traces or regenerated multiple-choice options.

\paragraph{Model-generated answers.} We freeze 200 distinct questions, 50 each from constructed Gordon, binomial calls, ordinary Gordon and WACC, selected by salted question-hash ranking after deduplication. Models receive only the question and common system instruction. The original three-model panel uses Qwen3 1.7B, Qwen2.5-Coder 3B and DeepSeek V3.2; a separately frozen Qwen3 4B extension adds 200 answers. Local models use greedy FP16 Apple MPS inference; DeepSeek uses a fixed SiliconFlow FP8 route with thinking and provider fallback disabled. All runs allow 1,024 output tokens. Checkpoints and provider differences preclude causal size/family claims.

Neither final-line endpoint checks every system instruction or the reasoning trace. Appendix~\ref{app:synthetic-model-protocol} gives exact freezes, settings, extractor rules and independent checks.

\paragraph{Wrong financial quantity.} For a one-period call, write $C_u=(Su-K)_+$, $C_d=(Sd-K)_+$, $R=1+r$, and $p=(R-d)/(u-d)$. The no-arbitrage price is
\begin{equation}  C=\frac{pC_u+(1-p)C_d}{R}=\Delta S-B_{\rm debt},\qquad B_{\rm debt}=\frac{\Delta Su-C_u}{R}. \end{equation} The independently inspected public
Cosimo source assigns $B_{\rm debt}$ as the call price. For $S=58,u=1.15,d=0.85,K=48,r=0.06$, this gives 45.28 instead of 12.72. The pinned release has 975 mismatches in 1,000 rows, or 888 among 905 distinct
questions. All 1,000 released golds match the financing-debt value within cent serialization. The other 25 numerical coincidences do not certify the generator's formula. Simple lower/upper call-price bounds detect none of the 1,000 source labels; two valuation identities are a stronger check.
The public source pin is not proven to be the exact generation commit of the dataset, but its formula reproduces the mechanism. The inspected verification harness regenerates the selected template from its seed
and compares answers~\cite{cosimosource}. That checks reproducibility against the same generator, allowing a common computational mistake to pass. Independent financial identities test a different property.

\paragraph{Hidden precision.} All 2,655 RLVR golds match their stored code's literal hidden operands within output serialization. All remain inside the
rounded-rate interval; its median relative width is 2.22\%. For CAPM, public source inspection identifies beta rounded from three to two decimals while the displayed rates are exact. All 1,000 golds are
feasible under beta $\pm0.005$ and cent-output serialization. The median relative beta-induced interval width is 0.632\%. These are rendering/identification findings. We do not call their gold values
intrinsically incorrect under rounded-input semantics.

\section{Related work and contribution boundary}
Label-error audits already establish evaluation sensitivity; reported ranking changes depend on the subset and noise setting~\cite{labelerrors}. Nonrandom noise, insufficient labeling information and NLU annotation ambiguity are established concerns~\cite{labelnoise,benchmarking}. FinanceReasoning repairs financial questions and labels with Python solutions~\cite{financereasoning}; FinChain checks units, precision and reasoning, including rounded visible inputs paired with full-precision computations~\cite{finchain}. FinVerBench studies observable information and rounded financial rendering~\cite{finverbench}. Arcifa and Carra's accessible abstract likewise requires model-visible answer determinacy; its reader-gated full text leaves exact experimental overlap unresolved~\cite{faircheap}. We claim no priority for those principles.

Measurement Risk studies financial rubric sensitivity~\cite{measurementrisk}; RewardHackingAgents separates reported from protected evaluation~\cite{rewardhackingagents}. Worker/LLM evaluation proxies and multiversion programming also expose correlated-error limits~\cite{aqer,independence}. Our specific evidence is a released debt-for-call numerical pattern and separately pinned generator/verifier computation, rather than a new general independence theorem.

FinQA, TAT-QA and BizBench establish document-based financial reasoning and program evaluation~\cite{finqa,tatqa,bizbench}. Program-of-Thoughts and PAL supply inexpensive interpreter baselines~\cite{pot,pal}; executable arithmetic alone does not identify the intended quantity. Zheng et al. document LLM mathematical grading failures and examine candidate-induced errors~\cite{llmjudge}, motivating separate same-model judge diagnostics rather than financial-truth claims. CheckList establishes targeted NLP behavioral tests~\cite{checklist}; future year/denominator/unit contrasts must distinguish answer-preserving edits from edits that change the answer.

Systematic verification errors already affect RLVR in controlled arithmetic studies~\cite{verifiernoise}, while reward-driven reflection can exploit flawed objectives~\cite{eposafe}. A recent verifier-error analysis studies fixed-verifier dynamics and weak-verifier arithmetic training under explicit assumptions~\cite{verifiererrors}. Learning consequences require a causal financial experiment beyond our unchanged-answer design; a tiny policy exploiting a known scalar collision would not establish that consequence. Our contribution is narrower: source-traced evidence about selected released training and preference supervision, passing-family controls, independently checked numerical patches and measured grading disagreements. Neither relabeling, interpreter use nor contract-aware verification is proposed as a new method.

\section{Limitations}
Mechanism-driven selection from two synthetic releases does not establish prevalence. Repeated templates and duplicate questions are dependent; passing families do not certify every reasoning trace. Manually specified formulas/parsers limit repair generality. Numerical patches are not regenerated multiple-choice options or certified training traces. Final-value collisions can hide wrong reasoning. Source-authored rejected answers and separate rational arithmetic supply additional finite checks, not general-validator performance.

No training, deployed upstream reward reproduction or human financial adjudication is completed. Checkpoints, specialization, formats, providers and quantization confound capacity comparisons. Exploratory extraction and convention sensitivities remain separate from strict endpoints. Saved-answer grading establishes scoring differences rather than learned harm.

Document references are AI-assisted and selected-case; shared conceptual errors, ambiguity, approximate inputs and pretraining exposure remain possible. Discovery-panel units lose semantic distinctions, native metrics use different precision conventions, and previously repaired derivative cases do not establish new-defect prevalence. Calculation representation confounds its initial reminder study; the follow-up removes that instruction difference, but does not supply expert truth or independently sourced document-benchmark breadth. The FinChain comparison adds one authoring pipeline and three current functions, without recovering historical instances or replicating the debt-for-call defect; different authorship does not prove statistical independence. The MATH500 census is grammar restricted, and its eight-question local subset contains seven integer references and one fraction. Authored controls and repeated generations do not estimate benchmark-wide comparator effects; the observed natural mathematical panel has no unequal-value credit. Hashes establish identity, not correctness or a complete research history. No sealed competition tasks or outcomes enter the audit.

\paragraph{Broader implications.} Source-traced audits can improve the accountability of financial supervision and evaluation. Applying numerical patches without preserving assumptions, units or legitimate conventions can instead erase defensible answers and introduce new grading errors. The supplied patches are numerical research evidence, not certified reasoning traces; effects on trained models or downstream financial decisions remain unmeasured.

\section{Conclusion}
In these selected financial supervision families, execution metadata can coexist with hidden precision, incorrect financial quantities, and omitted output instructions. Their interpretations differ: some
targets are wrong under the audited valuation identities and conventions, some violate a direct instruction, and others remain possible but unidentified. Source preference labels and reconstructed comparators make the
consequences observable. Release audits should therefore check visible inputs, the financial quantity, and requested output precision separately, while keeping numerical and trace validity distinct.
